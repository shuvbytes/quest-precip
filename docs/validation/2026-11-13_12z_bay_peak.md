# 260 mm in 3 hours: the Bay of Bengal peak in the 13 Nov 2026 12z run

**Question (user, 14 Nov):** 260 mm in one 3-hour window is extreme. Where is it, what do the neighbours show, and does
ECMWF's own chart for this run show a comparable peak?

**Answer:** it is a real single-cell burst in the model, inside a large cyclonic rain system over the west-central Bay.
ECMWF's charts for the same run show an instantaneous rate of more than 64 mm/h at the same spot at +123 h. They also show
the same pattern of 300–1000 mm six-day totals. The ensemble gives it a low chance. Not a processing error.

## The cell (our data: ECMWF Open Data, IFS HRES 0.25°, run 2026-11-13 12 UTC)
- **14.50°N 84.75°E**, open sea, west-central Bay of Bengal. Window **120–123 h** = Wed 18 Nov 2026 17:30 → 20:30 IST
  (12:00 → 15:00 UTC).
- 260.3 mm = TP(123 h) 421.4 mm − TP(120 h) 161.1 mm, i.e. a plain difference of two accumulated fields, with no clipping. The
  packing quantum at these steps is 0.488 mm (E = −11), so the error is under 1 mm.
- The 3×3 neighbourhood for that window (mm; north row first, west → east):

  ```
   24.9   73.2   42.5     14.75N
   78.6  260.3   19.5     14.50N
   85.9   99.6   15.6     14.25N
  ```
  The 8 neighbours range from 15.6 to 99.6 mm (median 57.9). The cell is 2.6× its wettest neighbour.
- The 3-hourly series at the cell from 105 h is 5.4, 6.8, 9.8, 5.9, 62.5, **260.3**, 40.5, 50.8, 4.9, 1.0, 0.5, 0.0, 0.5 mm.
  So it is one intense step, not a broken series.
- The 24 h total for 108–132 h is 441 mm (3×3: 81–552 mm). The 0–144 h total is 520 mm at the cell. The region maximum is
  1085 mm at 13.25°N 85.75°E.
- **Ensemble (same run, 108–132 h):** P(≥ 100 mm) is **4 %** at the cell (3×3: 2–8 %; region max 16 % at 13.00°N
  81.50°E). P(≥ 50 mm) is 28 %. HRES is an extreme member of the possible outcomes here. The map's "much wetter than the
  ensemble" hatching is meant for exactly this case.

## ECMWF's charts for the same run (OpenCharts, Southern Asia, base 2026-11-13 12 UTC)
Products `medium-rain-rate` (instantaneous total precipitation rate, mm/h) and `medium-rain-acc` (accumulated since the
start of the run). Both were downloaded on 14 Nov into `outputs/checks/ecmwf_13nov12z/` (git-ignored; ECMWF, CC BY 4.0).
- **Rate at +123 h** (15 UTC, the end of our window): one compact core in the west-central Bay with the chart's top
  classes, **> 64 mm/h** (7 pixels), inside 32–64 mm/h (6 px) and 16–32 mm/h. **At +120 h** the same spot is 32–64 mm/h.
  Our 260 mm in 3 h is a mean of 87 mm/h, so it is in the same class. Note that the chart shows instants and we show a
  3-hour total.
- **Position:** I placed the core by reading off the chart's 80°E meridian, the 10°N/20°N circles, the Chennai coast
  and the Andaman Islands. That gives about **14.7°N 84.4°E**, roughly 40 km from our cell. This is an eyeball estimate on a
  projected chart, not a fit.
- **Accumulation at +144 h** (legend: 100–300 dark red, **300–1000 magenta**): a small magenta blob at the core, a
  large magenta area to its south-east and a tiny speck to the west-north-west. Our 0–144 h cells ≥ 300 mm form the same
  three patches:

## Verdict
The peak is in ECMWF's own chart for this run, at the same place and time and of the same intensity class. Our value
is the model's grid-point value, not an artefact of de-accumulation or packing. It is still one model run's extreme
scenario: the ensemble gives ≥ 100 mm in that 24 h window a 4 % chance at this point. That is why the map shows the
ensemble chance next to the single run and marks the disagreement.
